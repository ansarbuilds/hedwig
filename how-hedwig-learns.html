Step 2 · What a knob is

A knob is just one number

Inside Hedwig there are numbers like 0.37 or -1.2. Each one nudges a guess a little. Turn it one way and "key" gets more likely; turn it the other way and "x" does. Engineers call these numbers parameters or weights. We call them knobs.

Step 6 · Why Mac wasn't enough & GPU speed

Why the Mac hit a wall — and why rented GPUs are 26× faster

Training work scales as FLOPs ≈ 6 × knobs × tokens. Reading the full 1.92 GB TinyStories shelf on a fanless MacBook Air M4 takes ~20 hours and heats up (throttling from 66s → 110s per 250 steps). Step 10 · What I learnt

Five lessons written down from real numbers

1

Change one thing at a time and measure it

Twice an upgrade hurt locally: 512-BPE overfit Tiny Shakespeare after step 750 (3.007 → 3.429), and RMSNorm ran 30% slower than LayerNorm on Apple mps (419 ms vs 323 ms) because Metal didn't fuse RMSNorm yet.

2

A rising score can be a lie (Reward Hacking)

Even at 14M knobs, pure DPO gamed the score by putting "Lily's" in every third word. Pairing preference loss with an SFT fluency anchor closed the loophole cleanly.

3

Reading beats knob count (Chinchilla in action)

286.7M won on multi-part stories, yet 97.6M (~1.7 tokens/knob) fixed "I goes" → "I go" better than under-read 286.7M (~0.7 tokens/knob, stopped at $1.70).

4

Compare stories, not raw scores across vocabs

4k, 8k, and 16k tokenizers create three different quiz scales. Reading real outputs side-by-side on fixed prompts is the only fair test.

5

Rehearse locally before renting cloud GPUs

Running --dry-run on the Mac for 92 seconds caught path and resume bugs for $0, so all three RunPod runs succeeded on the first try for $2.45 total.
