A field guide for new owl trainers

How Hedwig Learns

What a "knob" is, what "training" really does, and how a brain grows from one knob to 100 million. Play with the toys as you go.

Lesson 1 · The guessing game

An LLM only plays one game

It looks at some text and guesses what comes next. That's it. Hedwig guesses the next letter. Fable guesses the next word piece. Every answer you've ever gotten from a chatbot was built one guess at a time.

peanut butter and j_

Hedwig must guess the next letter. A smart guess is "e" (for "jelly").

Lesson 2 · What a knob is

A knob is just one number

Inside Hedwig there are numbers like 0.37 or -1.2. Each one nudges a guess a little. Turn it one way and "e" gets more likely; turn it the other way and "x" does. Engineers call these numbers parameters or weights. We call them knobs.

Try it: one knob, three guesses

Drag the slider. When the knob is set right, the green bar (the correct letter "e") is the biggest. That means Hedwig guesses well.

Lesson 3 · What training is

Training means turning the knobs, automatically, millions of times

You turned the knob by hand. Training does the same thing, but the computer does it, for every knob at once, over and over. Here's one round:

1 · Read

Show a snippet

Take 128 letters of Shakespeare.

2 · Guess

Predict

Guess each next letter using today's knobs.

3 · Check

Score it

Compare with the real letters. How wrong was it?

4 · Nudge

Turn knobs

Turn every knob a tiny bit in the direction that would've been less wrong.

Try it: let the computer train the knob

Step 0 · knob -2.0 · wrongness 2.31

Each step checks how wrong the guess was and nudges the knob a little. Watch the dial above move and the green bar grow. This "how wrong" number is exactly the test score you see in Hedwig's training tab.

The secret trick: the math tells the computer which way to turn each knob without trying both ways. It's called backpropagation. It's why we can tune millions of knobs instead of just one.

Lesson 5 · Where the 4.8 million knobs live

Knobs come in groups, like Lego bricks

Nobody places 4.8 million knobs one by one. You pick a few settings (how many layers, how big each layer is), and the knobs appear automatically. Here's Hedwig's brain, counted from its real settings:

Part of the brainWhat it doesKnobs
Letter dictionaryTurns each of 65 letters into a list of 256 numbers16,640
Position tagsRemembers where each letter sits (1st, 2nd … 128th)32,768
6 thinking layers"Attention" (which earlier letters matter?) + "thinking" (mix it all up). ~790k each.4,738,560
Final guesserTurns the thoughts into a score for each of 65 letters17,217
Total≈ 4,805,185
The growth rule: make each layer twice as wide and you get about 4× the knobs. Stack twice as many layers and you get 2×. That's how you go from Hedwig to GPT-2: same Lego bricks, bigger and more of them.

Recap

The whole thing in four lines

1. A knob is one number inside the brain.
2. The brain's job is to guess the next letter.
3. Training = guess, check how wrong, nudge every knob a little, repeat thousands of times.
4. More knobs + more reading = a smarter brain, but it needs a bigger computer.
